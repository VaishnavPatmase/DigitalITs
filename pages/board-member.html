A quiet conviction about how this industry should work.

A personal note on building Digital It Move, the kind of firm we wished existed when we were the ones buying IT services and the standards we hold ourselves to.

Prathmesh Patmase
Board Member · Digital It Move

Most IT services firms are, at heart, staffing businesses. They sell hours. They hire smart people, mark them up, and hope the difference between the client's problem and the firm's bench doesn't show up in the invoice. Some of them are very good at it. Almost none of them are what enterprise technology actually needs.

Digital It Move was founded on a different premise that the value an IT partner delivers is measured by outcomes shipped, not hours billed. The hours are a means. The shipped system, the resolved incident, the closed audit, the boardroom that finally works the way it should those are the unit of work. Everything else is overhead.

This isn't a marketing position. It's a structural one. It changes who we hire, how we price, which engagements we accept, and how we hand things off. It is also, I should be clear about this, the harder way to run a firm and the only way I'd want to run one.

What we believe

Three convictions sit underneath everything we do. They are not original. They are not particularly fashionable. They have, however, served our clients well for the better part of a decade, and I see no reason to retire them.

1. Senior people, doing the work.

We do not staff junior associates on enterprise engagements and hope nobody notices. The person who scopes your project is the person who builds it, runs it, and answers the phone when it goes sideways at 2am. The economics of staff augmentation are seductive for a services firm. They are corrosive to the work. We have made our choice.

2. You own what we build.

No proprietary lock-in. No licensing games. No "you can't migrate off this because we built it on our special framework." If you part ways with us tomorrow, your systems keep running, your data stays yours, and your team has the documentation to operate what we delivered. Anything less is, in our view, a form of leverage that doesn't belong in a partnership.

3. Outcome-priced, where we can be.

Where the outcome is measurable, we will price against it. ROAS, MTTC, uptime, audit pass, relocation cutover window. When the outcome is harder to pin down, we agree the success metrics in writing before any line is shipped. This aligns our incentives with yours in a way that hourly billing never can.

The industry sells hours because it's easy to count. The interesting question the one that determines whether a partnership actually works is what was produced for those hours. We try to keep that question at the centre of every engagement. Prathmesh Patmase, on how we scope

Why "integrated" is the word we keep using

Most enterprises we work with are not struggling with one technology problem. They are struggling with the coordination tax of having five, six, sometimes ten specialist vendors each excellent in their lane, none of them talking to each other. The security team's detection rule doesn't know what the network team just changed. The cloud team's migration broke something the marketing team's analytics platform depended on. Nobody's fault, everyone's problem.

We are structured to be the answer to that. Eleven practices, one team, one P&L, one phone number. When you bring us in, you don't have to translate between vendors. We already speak each other's languages. That sounds like a small thing. In practice, it is the difference between a project that takes six months and one that takes nine and between an architecture that holds up under load and one that needs a refresh in eighteen months.

What we are not, in the interest of clarity

  • We are not the cheapest. We are priced for the value of senior delivery, not the volume of junior hours.
  • We are not the largest. We are deliberately scoped to the engagements we can run well.
  • We are not a fit for everyone. If your priority is the lowest possible invoice, we will tell you so and point you to someone who can compete on that axis.
  • We are not infallible. We will tell you when we are wrong, when the approach isn't working, and when the brief itself is the problem.

What I would like you to take from this

If you are reading this, you are likely considering Digital It Move for something a migration, a security programme, a re-engineered boardroom, a relocation, a complete IT reset. I cannot tell you from this page whether we are the right team. I can tell you what we will bring if we are: senior engineers who own the outcome, an integrated approach that removes the coordination tax, and a willingness to be honest about scope, risk, and cost at every stage of the engagement.

If that is the kind of partnership you are looking for, the next step is a 20-minute conversation. No slides, no procurement theatre. Just a real discussion of what you are trying to achieve.

I look forward to it.
